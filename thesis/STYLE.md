# Writing style for this thesis

Binding style contract for all prose in this repository. Applies to `.tex` files
under `chapters/`, `appendix/`, and the abstract. Does not apply to code comments
or commit messages.

## 0. What the assistant may and may not do

- May: restructure, tighten, harmonize terminology, fix tense and agreement,
  propose alternative phrasings, flag unsupported claims, check that a sentence
  matches the table or figure it refers to.
- May not: invent citations, invent numerical results, soften or strengthen a
  claim beyond what the cited evidence supports, or fill a gap in the argument
  with plausible-sounding filler.
- When a passage cannot be fixed without information the assistant does not have,
  insert `% TODO(style): <what is missing>` and leave the prose alone. Do not
  guess.
- Never rewrite a passage that already satisfies this document just to make it
  smoother.

## 1. Register and voice

- Academic English, dry and declarative. No warm-up sentences, no rhetorical
  questions, no addressing the reader.
- First person plural (`we`) for methodological choices and for guiding the
  reader: "we decompose the instance into k subclusters", "we report crossing
  times rather than fixed-horizon gaps". First person singular only in the
  acknowledgments.
- Never "I believe", "I think", "in my view", "we feel". A thesis asserts or
  cites. If the support is weak, say what the support is: "the evidence covers
  three instances and does not separate the two explanations".
- Passive voice is acceptable where the agent is genuinely irrelevant ("the
  instances were generated by Queiroga et al."), but active voice is the default.
- Do not hedge reflexively. "This suggests" is appropriate when the evidence is
  indirect. "This may possibly indicate" is not.

## 2. Claims and evidence

- Every quantitative statement in prose must be traceable to a specific table,
  figure, or cited source. If a number appears in the text, the reader must be
  able to find where it came from.
- Distinguish three registers explicitly and never blur them:
  1. observed ("BG-AILS reduced the mean gap by 0.14 pp on the mid-size subset"),
  2. inferred ("the increased variance is consistent with repeated selection of
     the same boundary pair"),
  3. speculative ("a replacement-aware selection rule might remove this effect;
     this was not tested").
- Confounds and deviations are stated in the running text where the affected
  result is presented, not deferred to a footnote or a limitations section at the
  end. A result presented without its known confound is a defect.
- No superlatives without a comparison set. "State of the art" means the specific
  published baselines named in Chapter 2, and nothing else.

## 3. Terminology

Fixed spellings and capitalization. Never introduce a synonym for a defined term.

| Use | Not |
| --- | --- |
| DRISPI | Drispi, the framework, our solver |
| HAOS | the bandit, the controller (after first definition) |
| BG-AILS | BG AILS, boundary AILS |
| AILS-II | AILS2, AILS II |
| the DRI loop | the main loop, the outer loop |
| set covering / set partitioning (SC/SP) | set-cover, SCP |
| subcluster | sub-cluster, cluster (when a subcluster is meant) |
| subsolver | sub-solver, inner solver |
| instance | problem, testcase |

Notation traps:

- `k` in a Queiroga instance name is the vehicle count fixed by the benchmark
  set. The decomposition cluster count is also written `k`. Whenever both could
  be meant in the same paragraph, disambiguate on first use and keep the
  distinction visible thereafter.
- `n` is the customer count, excluding the depot. State this once, then rely on it.
- Wall-clock seconds, not "time", when a duration is meant.

## 4. Structure

- Paragraphs carry one point each. If a paragraph has two, split it.
- Do not open every section with a preview sentence and close it with a summary
  sentence. Vary. Some sections can end on their last substantive point.
- Do not impose a uniform shape (setup, three items, conclusion) across sections.
  Uniform shape reads as generated.
- Forward references only where genuinely needed. "As we will see in Chapter 5"
  is usually a symptom of material in the wrong place.

## 5. Mechanics

- **American spelling throughout.** `-ize` not `-ise`, `analyze` not `analyse`,
  `behavior` not `behaviour`, `center` not `centre`, `modeling` not `modelling`.
  This includes figure captions, table headers, and the abstract. Quoted titles
  in the bibliography keep their original spelling.
- **Sentence spacing is handled in the output, not the source.**  This template
  runs without `\frenchspacing`, so TeX already sets a wider gap after
  sentence-ending punctuation in the PDF.  Write a single space after a period in
  the source; runs of spaces are collapsed and have no effect.  The two escapes
  that make the wider gap land in the right places are in §8 and are mandatory.
- Paragraphs are wrapped as normal prose.  There is no one-sentence-per-line
  convention.
- **Serial comma.** Always, before the final conjunction in a list of three or
  more items: "vertex-based clustering, route-based clustering, and the hybrid
  variant". No exceptions, including in figure captions and table headers.
- Single space after a comma, colon, or semicolon.
- Numbers under ten are spelled out in prose, except when they carry a unit or
  refer to a labeled quantity: "three seeds", but "3 cores", "k = 8".
- No contractions.

## 6. Vocabulary and constructions

Banned outright. These are the patterns that make prose read as machine-written,
plus terms with no place in a technical thesis. Replacements are given, because
removing a word without offering a substitute produces something worse.

### 6.1 Openers and connectives

| Banned | Instead |
| --- | --- |
| "However" as a sentence opener | Recast, or use "but" mid-sentence, or move the contrast into the clause |
| "Additionally", especially as an opener | Delete, or state the actual relation ("the same run also showed") |
| "Furthermore", "Moreover" as filler | Delete |
| "In summary", "Overall, it can be said", "It is important to note", "It is worth mentioning" | Delete and state the point |
| "the former", "the latter" | Repeat the noun |
| "the fact that" | Recast: "the fact that k was too large" becomes "the oversized k" |
| "due to" | "because of", or recast as a causal clause |
| "in order to" | "to" |

### 6.2 Reporting verbs

- Never "stated". For what a source does: reports, shows, finds, argues,
  proposes, measures, observes. Choose the one that is true; "argues" and
  "shows" are not interchangeable.
- Banned as verbs: highlight, underscore, emphasize, showcase, garner, boast
  (meaning "has"), bolster, delve, foster, cultivate, encompass, align with,
  resonate with, serve as, stand as.
- Replacements: shows, indicates, contains, includes, supports, matches,
  increases, has, is.

### 6.3 Inflated evaluatives

Banned adjectives and adverbs: vital, crucial, pivotal, key (as adjective),
essential (unless logically necessary, and shown to be), valuable, meticulous,
meticulously, intricate, vibrant, enduring, lasting, ongoing, novel,
comprehensive, robust (unless defined), powerful, seamless, significant (unless
statistically significant, in which case name the test).

If a quantity matters, say how much and to what. "The pool cap matters" is empty.
"Raising the pool cap above 10,000 columns increased SP solve time by 40% with no
gap improvement" is not.

### 6.4 Metaphor and cliché nouns

Banned: landscape (as an abstract noun for a field or situation), tapestry,
testament, reminder, interplay, intricacies, focal point, deep dive, indelible
mark, turning point, valuable insights, evolving landscape.

Banned phrases built from these: "is a testament to", "is a reminder that",
"serves as", "stands as", "plays a key role", "reflects broader", "deeply
rooted", "represents a shift", "marks a shift", "sets the stage for", "shaping
the", "marking the", "symbolizing its ongoing/enduring/lasting".

### 6.5 Trailing participial clauses

The pattern is a main clause, then a comma, then an `-ing` verb that editorializes
about what the main clause means. Banned in this position: underscoring,
emphasizing, highlighting, reflecting, symbolizing, demonstrating, ensuring,
cultivating, fostering, encompassing, showcasing.

Bad: "The gap fell to 0.19%, underscoring the effectiveness of the decomposition."
Good: "The gap fell to 0.19%." If the interpretation is worth stating, give it its
own sentence and mark its register per §2.

Participles are fine where they carry content: "the solver terminated after
3600 seconds, having exhausted the iteration budget".

### 6.6 Constructions

- "not only X but also Y": banned.
- "not X, but Y": at most once per chapter, and only where the contrast is the
  substantive point rather than a rhetorical flourish. Prefer stating Y directly.
- "Despite its X, Y faces several challenges", "Despite these challenges":
  banned. Name the specific limitation and its consequence.
- Section headings named "Challenges and Legacy", "Future Outlook", "Key
  Takeaways": banned. Headings describe content: "Limitations of the boundary
  perturbation", "Untested extensions".
- Three-item lists assembled for rhythm rather than because there are three
  things. Two or four is often the honest count.
- Em dashes. Use a comma, a colon, parentheses, or a full stop.
- Sentences that resolve symmetrically in the same rhythm as their neighbors.
  Vary sentence length deliberately.

### 6.7 Watch list

Not banned. Flag each occurrence for manual review rather than replacing it
silently:

- "associated with", "in association with": legitimate where a statistical
  association is meant and causation is not claimed. Suspect everywhere else.
- "connected with", "in connection with", "related to": usually vagueness
  standing in for a specific relation. Name the relation.
- "correlates with": only with a coefficient and a test.

### 6.8 Carve-outs

The bans in §6.2, §6.3, and §6.4 apply to prose register, not to established
technical vocabulary. These remain available in their precise senses:

- *fitness landscape*, *search landscape*: standard metaheuristics terms
- *key* as a data-structure key, sort key, or dictionary key
- *highlight* in a figure caption describing visual emphasis ("the shaded band
  highlights the 95% CI")
- *significant* in "statistically significant", with the test named
- *robust* in "robust statistics", "robust to outliers"
- *encompass* only in a set-theoretic sense where "contains" would be wrong

Test for whether a use is technical or rhetorical: if replacing the word with a
plain synonym loses precision, it is technical. If it loses nothing, it was
rhetorical.

## 7. Tense

- Present tense for the properties of the method, the structure of the document,
  and established facts: "HAOS maintains six roulette wheels", "Chapter 4
  describes the benchmark design".
- Past tense for what was done in the experiments and what was observed: "we ran
  three seeds per instance", "the batch effect exceeded the sampling variance".
- Present perfect only for the state of the literature: "prior work has focused
  on single-node solvers".

## 8. LaTeX conventions

**The template is authoritative.**  Where the thesis template, its class or style
files, or the existing chapters use a convention that differs from anything in
this section, follow the template.  This section describes defaults for cases the
template leaves open, not a standard to impose on it.

Before editing any `.tex` file:

1. Read `settings.tex` and `main.tex`.  The subsection below records the current
   state, but the files are the source of truth and may have moved on.
2. Read one representative existing chapter.  Match its label scheme, its
   citation commands, its table style, and its math conventions.
3. If the template defines a custom macro for something, use the macro rather
   than writing the underlying markup by hand.
4. Never load a package the template does not already load.  If a construction
   seems to need one, insert `% TODO(style): needs <package>` and stop.
5. If the template and an existing chapter disagree, follow the template and flag
   the chapter with `% TODO(style): deviates from template`.  Do not silently
   rewrite it.

### This template

`scrreprt` (KOMA-Script), A4, 12pt, compiled with pdflatex through `latexmk`.
Configuration lives in `main.tex` and `settings.tex`; cover metadata in
`info.tex`.  What follows is binding for this project.

**Available.**  `natbib` with round brackets and a custom `biblio_style.bst`,
`glossaries` with acronym and symbol lists, `booktabs`, `algorithm2e`,
`listings`, `tikz`/`pgfplots`, `float`, `rotating`, `amsmath`, `hyperref`,
`babel` (English), `setspace`.

**Not available.**  `cleveref` and `siunitx` are not loaded.  Do not write
`\cref`, `\Cref`, `\SI`, `\si`, or `\num`.  If a construction seems to require
either package, insert `% TODO(style): needs <package>` and leave the passage
alone.

- **Citations.**  `\citet` when the authors are the sentence subject, `\citep`
  otherwise.  Never a bare `\cite`.  Sources go in `bibliography/literature.bib`;
  never add an entry without a verifiable DOI, URL, or ISBN.
- **Cross-references.**  `\ref` with an explicit non-breaking prefix, matching
  whatever the existing chapters already do: `Table~\ref{tab:xl-summary}`,
  `Section~\ref{sec:haos}`.  Since `hyperref` is loaded, `\autoref` is an
  alternative, but pick one and use it everywhere rather than mixing.
- **Labels** prefixed by type: `sec:`, `fig:`, `tab:`, `eq:`, `alg:`, `app:`,
  with lowercase hyphenated text: `\label{fig:crossing-time-xl}`.
- **Acronyms.**  The template loads `glossaries` with an acronym list.  Use
  `\gls{}` and the related commands rather than typing an expansion by hand, so
  first use expands automatically and the list stays complete.  This applies to
  CVRP, HAOS, BG-AILS, SC/SP, and every other acronym in §3.
- **Units and numbers.**  Written out, since `siunitx` is absent: "3600 seconds",
  "10,000 columns", "0.213%".  Percentage points are `pp`, percentages are `\%`.
  Never mix the two.  Keep a non-breaking space where a wrap would be wrong:
  `k~=~8`, `3600~seconds`.
- **Tables** through `booktabs`: `\toprule`, `\midrule`, `\bottomrule`, no
  vertical rules.  Wide result tables use `rotating` rather than shrinking the
  font below the body size.
- **Algorithms** through `algorithm2e`, **code** through `listings`, **plots**
  through `pgfplots`.  Do not introduce a second mechanism for any of these.
- **Math:** variables italic, operators upright, multi-letter identifiers in
  `\mathrm{}`.
- **Paragraphs** are separated by a blank line.  The template sets no indent and
  a 6pt paragraph skip, so the break is already visible.  Never use `\\` for a
  line break in running text, and never `\vspace` to fix layout.
- **Sentence spacing in the output.**  `\frenchspacing` is OFF in this template
  (verified: `\sfcode`\.` is 3000).  Sentence breaks therefore receive a wider
  gap than word spaces in the PDF.  This is independent of the two-space source
  convention in §5, which TeX collapses and which affects only the source file.
  Two consequences are binding:
  - **Abbreviations mid-sentence** must suppress the sentence gap with an
    explicit space: `e.g.,\ `, `i.e.,\ `, `cf.\ `, `vs.\ `, `et al.\ (2026)`.
    Without it the period is read as a sentence end and the gap is too wide.
    Check every `et al.` that appears outside a `\citet`/`\citep` call.
  - **Sentences ending in a capital letter** must force the sentence gap with
    `\@` before the period, because uppercase letters suppress it:
    `... outperformed by AILS-II\@.`, `... resolved in the SC/SP\@.`,
    `... reported for HGS\@.`  This applies to every acronym in §3 and is the
    more common of the two errors in this document.
  - Recorded alternative: adding `\frenchspacing` to `settings.tex` makes both
    rules unnecessary by giving uniform spacing throughout.  If that is ever
    adopted, delete these two bullets rather than leaving dead rules in place.

## 9. Examples

Before:

> It is important to note that our novel decomposition approach delivers
> significant improvements across a comprehensive range of instances, and
> experts agree that adaptive control is a crucial component of modern
> metaheuristics.

After:

> The decomposition reduced the mean gap on 71 of the 100 XL instances
> (\Cref{tab:xl-summary}).  The effect is largest above 5000 customers, where the
> subcluster count reaches the cap of 14.

Before:

> The results are promising and demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed
> framework, which is not only faster but also more robust than the baseline.

After:

> DRISPI reaches the published two-hour reference quality of AILS-II after 4100
> seconds on average.  Whether the advantage persists at longer horizons was not
> tested; the campaign was capped at eight hours.

Before:

> However, due to the fact that the former method produced degenerate partitions,
> we adopted the latter, underscoring the importance of clustering quality.

After:

> Single-linkage agglomerative clustering produced degenerate partitions, with a
> median maximum cluster share of 0.997 (\Cref{tab:clustering-degeneracy}).  We
> therefore used k-medoids for the remaining experiments.
