\begin{abstract}\thispagestyle{scrheadings}
    Parcel carriers route thousands of customers per depot within hours, straining monolithic solvers for capacitated vehicle routing. Decomposition into subproblems that existing solvers handle in parallel eases that strain, but the best decomposition and subsolver vary by instance. This thesis develops DRISPI, a framework that recombines subproblem routes by set partitioning. Its controller, HAOS, is meant to learn these choices from observed costs. Its central improvement step, BG-AILS, focuses perturbation and local search on subcluster boundaries, where decomposition leaves structural defects. On the 100 XL instances, using eight cores within a two-hour wall-clock limit, DRISPI significantly beats five of eight published solvers, is indistinguishable from FILO and FILO2, and trails only AILS-II, the strongest of them. BG-AILS outperforms unguided perturbation and accounts for 88\% of the cost reduction after the initial solution. HAOS shows no sign of learning: its weights follow sampling noise, not option performance or instance structure.
\end{abstract}
